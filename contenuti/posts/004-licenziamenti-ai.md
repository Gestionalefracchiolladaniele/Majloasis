# Post 004 — "Quello che i 'licenziamenti AI' dicono davvero sull'azienda"

> Bucket **Growth** (hot take). Adattamento fedele del post Reddit: stessa tesi e stessa

> Tema che il pubblico AI sta già guardando → si aggancia un'opinione netta. **Safe:** zero

## Testo (pronto da pubblicare)

```
Most "AI layoffs" are not about AI.

There are only two ways a company can compete.

Do the same thing as everyone else, but cheaper. Or do something better, at a reasonable cost.

Most layoffs sold as "AI" are just the first one, wearing a futuristic costume.

Anyone who has actually used AI for real work knows you can't leave it running unsupervised. Yet that's exactly what a lot of companies are doing now. Sometimes you can tell at a glance: AI tools running, no human in the loop.

So the value equation tilts toward cutting costs, not getting better.

That's how you get expensive mistakes.

If there's one thing I'm sure of, it's this. Keep your people and augment them with AI. Don't cut headcount to replace a human with a tool.

The companies that win won't be the cheapest.

They'll be the ones who made their people 10x.
```
