# Post 005 — "Se il 100% dei CIO investe nell'AI, perché il dibattito la dipinge come un flop?"

> Bucket **Growth** (newsjacking + hot take). Adattamento fedele dello Screen 2: stessi dati,
> stessa tesi (il dibattito pubblico e l'adozione aziendale stanno divergendo). Solo ripulito
> per LinkedIn. Cita la fonte RBC/Business Insider → credibilità. **Safe:** zero progetti/scraping.

## Testo (pronto da pubblicare)

```
100% of surveyed CIOs plan to invest in AI. The public debate still calls it a failed experiment.

RBC surveyed 100+ CIOs and tech leaders. The numbers:

~90% say token budgets are manageable.
More than half already have AI in production.
Another 35% plan to ship within 6 months.
100% have budget allocated for AI projects.
The "SaaSpocalypse" everyone predicted hasn't happened.

This is the opposite of the online story, where AI is a passing fad, pilots are failing, and everyone is about to back out.

Here's what I think is going on. The consumer AI debate and the enterprise AI debate have split apart.

The public one is stuck on bad chatbots, hallucinations, model drama.

Meanwhile companies, quietly, are turning AI into a budget line. Then a workflow layer. Eventually a pricing model.

That doesn't mean there's no bubble. It means the question should move from "is anyone actually using this?" to "who gets real value, and does the ROI justify the spend?"

Maybe we're underrating enterprise adoption because the consumer product still feels chaotic.

Source in the comments.
```
