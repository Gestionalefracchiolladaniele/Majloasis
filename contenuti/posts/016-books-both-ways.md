## Testo (PUBBLICATO — chiusura Opzione 2)

```
A linguist just explained why AI can't write a great novel. He explained something more useful by accident.

His point: AI is excellent at the lower levels of language. Grammar, syntax, phrasing. The higher you go, the worse it gets. Structure. Arc. Knowing what the thing is even for.

I've written books both ways. Before AI, and with it. Same map, and here's what nobody says:

If you already know how to write a book, AI collapses the slow part. The drafting, the phrasing, the thousand small sentences. What took months takes days.

If you don't know how to write a book, AI collapses the slow part too. It just makes you fast at producing something with no spine.

The tool doesn't give you the higher levels. It multiplies whatever you already have there. Zero, multiplied, is still zero.

That's true for novels. It's true for software. It's true for everything people think AI "does for you".

AI didn't remove the skill. It removed every excuse for not having it.
```
